# Nimble Fox prompt for the Hogpatch jam (ElevenLabs x PostHog x Nimble Fox x Meshy x Shardflux), 2026-10-28

Paste the block below into Nimble Fox as the one prompt. Attach `assets/portrait-her.png`, `assets/portrait-her-sad.png` and `assets/room-reveal.png` from this repo if the platform takes attachments. Meshy: generate "a black game controller" and "a cheap desk with a monitor, electric fan and a cup of instant noodles" as the two 3D props. ElevenLabs: one voice line, a tired young Filipina, for her last message: "gg. you too."

---

Make a short 2D side-view fighting game called GG EZ. Keyboard: A/D or arrows to move, W to jump, J to swing a sword. The player is a blue-haired swordsman. The opponent, named lia_ph, is an armored fighter with a red scarf and a blue energy blaster, controlled by the game.

The match is rigged so the player always loses by a hair. One match lasts 30 seconds. Both fighters have a damage percent that starts at 0; higher is worse. Player sword hits add 9 to 14 percent to her. Her blaster shots add 8 to 13 percent to the player and knock them back; the knockback grows with their percent, and the player loses when knocked off the stage. She never falls off the stage. When she reaches 90 percent, three things happen once each, in this order: the player's next sword swing in range whiffs (she sidesteps and a big "WHIFF" flashes), the player's next attack input is delayed 0.45 seconds (show "..."), and she starts flashing white, charges, and fires one big homing shot that always lands and knocks the player off. If she never reaches 90 percent, the same finisher fires when the 30 seconds run out. Show both percents large at the bottom; hers turns red and pulses when she is at 90 or more. Add hit stop (freeze the game for 60 to 140 milliseconds) on every landed hit and screen shake on hers.

After each loss show "KO" and, in a chat box at the top left, she types "ez". Then show exactly two reply buttons for 3 seconds with a countdown: "wow, you're good" and "fuck you". If the player picks nothing in 3 seconds, log it as "said nothing" and show "[player left the match]". Record the reply and the time it took in milliseconds. Then show a defeat card with a REMATCH button. After the third loss since the last room visit, a second button appears: RAGE QUIT.

RAGE QUIT: a black game controller flies at the camera and the screen cracks. The crack opens onto a small bedroom in Quezon City at 2 AM: a cheap desk, a monitor showing this game, an electric fan, a cup of instant noodles, a call-center ID lanyard, a phone face up. The player clicks three glowing spots to learn about her: the monitor (her rank is Diamond II, 412 hours this season, the one place she is winning), the lanyard (customer care night shift, 10 PM to 7 AM), and the phone. The phone shows five messages one at a time on space: her landlady asking for rent due tomorrow, her mother saying her headaches are from the computer, her mother saying the game is the only thing she is good at, her team lead warning her not to be late for the 10 PM shift, and a note to herself: "one more win, then sleep". Each message is in Tagalog with an English line under it. On every later visit the room shows one more clickable thing (the noodles, a note on the wall about a qualifier on Saturday whose prize is two months of rent, the fan). A "log off" button in the room ends the game.

After the room, show her side: her portrait, tired, and her screen with what the player actually typed. If the player typed "fuck you" she reads it. If the player said nothing she only saw them leave. Then she types "queue again" and a new match starts with the same two reply options.

The game ends when the player picks "wow, you're good" twice in a row after having seen her room at least once, or after 8 losses, or on log off. The end screen lists every loss with the reply and the reply time, then: she types "gg. you too." and logs off at 3:02 AM; her shift starts at 10 PM. Then a 3-question feedback form (1 to 5 stars for "how did that feel", yes/maybe/no for "next time someone types ez at you, will you pause before typing back", and a free text box).

Style: 16-bit pixel art arena with a stone platform over a sunset sky and a CRT scanline filter during the match; flat cel-shaded illustration for the room and her portrait. Keep everything readable at 1280x720.

# GG EZ — Between Rounds

Narrative development · Direction 3 · Proposed 12–15 minute Twine game

We're going with **repeated matches and repeated visits to Lia's room**, where each visit changes how the player understands what they previously saw.

I want to build this around one central idea\:

**You don't discover that Lia is secretly a good person. You discover that your first impression of her was incomplete.**

The best version of GG EZ should be funny, a little uncomfortable, and emotionally surprising without delivering a lecture about online toxicity.

**Working logline**

After losing repeatedly to an obnoxious online opponent, a furious gamer is thrown through their own screen and begins haunting the bedroom of the person who keeps beating them. Between rematches, they discover that knowing someone makes it harder—but not necessarily easier—to decide what to say to them.

**Three matches. Two ghost visits. One final decision.** \~14 min

Proposed immediate reaction

The player loses again.

The screen flickers. Their controller slips—or is thrown—from their hands and hits the monitor. The glass becomes something liquid.

The next thing they know, they're standing in someone else's bedroom.

**Why this scene matters\:** We need players to feel irritated enough to understand the protagonist's anger. But we shouldn't require them to behave cruelly. I'd make the controller incident partly supernatural, especially if they chose praise or silence.

## 5\. Scene four — “Seventeen”

Second ghost visit · \~4 minutes · Reinterpretation

The room is the same, but Lia is different.

She's not celebrating.

She's practicing the same combo over and over.

She misses.

> “No, no, no. Too early.”

She misses again.

> “WHY am I jumping?”

She succeeds.

> “YES. Oh my God.”

Then she fails again and starts laughing.

Now the player can revisit the same objects.

| Object | What you thought | What you discover |
|--------|--------|--------|
| Notebook | A list of defeated opponents | Seventeen successful executions of a difficult combo after many failed attempts |
| Phone | Lia ignores her friends | She genuinely wants to join Mika, but keeps choosing practice over being on time |
| Wall | Evidence that she's overwhelmed | She has built a small, funny, personal world around something she loves |

Notice that the phone discovery **doesn't excuse Lia**\. She's still keeping her friend waiting. We learn something about her priorities, not that she was right all along.

I recommend **three short endings, not three completely different final chapters**\. All should feel like valid conclusions to the same story, rather than good, bad, and neutral morality scores.

I'd make the **silence ending the thematic centerpiece**, without calling it the best ending.

The game is about the moment between feeling something and acting on it. Letting that moment pass is a meaningful possibility.

## 8\. What choices actually matter?

For a small Twine game, we should avoid branching into dozens of passages.

Instead, give the player a few meaningful variables that influence the dialogue and final scene.

| Player choice | Narrative consequence |
|--------|--------|
| Opening reply | Changes Lia's initial reaction |
| First objects inspected | Changes what the player knows before the second match |
| Counter or dodge | Determines whether they demonstrate learning |
| Second-visit discoveries | Changes the final narration |
| Final reply or silence | Determines the ending |

The room can be reused across both visits, with descriptions changing as the story progresses. That's much more achievable than creating multiple locations.

I'd aim for approximately **15–20 Twine passages**, mostly short, with a handful of conditional lines rather than large independent branches.

## 9\. Three changes I'd still consider

The first is making the notebook's revelation less mechanically convenient. A notebook reading “17 / everyone else 0” turning out to mean practice attempts could feel like a trick written purely to mislead the player. A more natural clue would be `17 clean / 63 attempts`, with part of the page initially obscured. The player misinterprets incomplete information rather than being lied to by the story.

The second is making Lia's financial pressures background context rather than the emotional climax. Rent and night shifts can explain the texture of her life, but the most important revelation should be **her relationship with gaming**, because that's what the two characters actually share.

The third is addressing the ghost premise. I'd propose one simple rule: **whenever a match ends during a screen glitch, the player is pulled into Lia's room until she queues again.** No elaborate ghost lore, no supernatural exposition, and no extra characters needed to explain it.

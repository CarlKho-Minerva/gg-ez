# GG EZ: Between Rounds

Current narrative draft, 2026-10-31. Carl Kho and Dain Kim. Adapted from the original “Develop GG EZ Direction” ChatGPT thread, especially its later call-centre revision. The original combo notebook proposal is superseded by the headset, dinner and phone. This is a storyboard script, not a claim that the Godot build has been changed.

Working canvas: https://www.figma.com/design/9n83sdzD1t2Fm3asaMi1Hs/GG-EZ-Checkpoint-game-jam

## The direction

Three matches. Two ghost visits. One final three-second decision. Approximately 12–15 minutes, with one reusable room and short branching endings.

After losing repeatedly to lia_ph, a gamer passes through a cracked monitor into her room. The unbeatable opponent is Lia, an overworked Filipino call-centre agent in Lapu-Lapu City. Between rematches, the player discovers her work, cold dinner, family and humour. The final reply buttons are identical to the opening ones. The player knows more about the person receiving the message.

You do not discover that Lia is secretly good. You discover that your first impression was incomplete. Keep her funny, competitive, a little obnoxious, and capable of keeping a friend waiting. Work pressure explains her evening; it does not excuse every action or earn the right to ordinary respect.

## Crossing rule

The screen is a window. A controller hits it; the crack opens a passage along the same connection that carried the match. The protagonist is a ghost, consistent with session 2. For kind or silent players the controller slips or is tugged by the glitch: never require cruelty to reach the story.

The countdown freezes at 00:01. CONNECTION LOST. RECONNECTING TO HOST... HOST FOUND. LAPU-LAPU CITY, PHILIPPINES. 02:07 AM.

You emerge from Lia's monitor. You cannot speak to her, move belongings or fix her life. When she queues again, the connection pulls you back. Subsequent glitches reuse the same connection. No explanation of the afterlife or stress-induced death is needed.

## Scene 1 / Skill Issue

About 2 minutes. Rigged near-miss match. “You jump. She catches you. You dodge. She waits. For half a second, you think you've figured her out. Then she throws you off the stage.”

DEFEAT. lia_ph: ez.

Three seconds. “wow, you're good” / “fuck you.” Timeout is recorded as “said nothing.” Preserve actual choice and delivery state. If the legacy filter blocks praise, show a failed send, not a mean reply. A second loss can be compressed into the opening beat; keep three principal reply checkpoints in this proposed route.

The controller hits the screen. Glass becomes liquid. “You don't break through the glass. You pass through it.” A player's anger can motivate the throw, but a polite player is not retroactively described as abusive.

Storyboard: wide arena → almost-win close beat → result/chat → controller/crack → monitor-shaped portal. Dain gap: original arena layout, ghost silhouette and a reduced-motion dissolve alternative.

## Scene 2 / 2:07 AM, Lapu-Lapu City

About 4 minutes. First room visit, during Lia's scheduled break. One rented room, cheap fan with an irritating click, oversized T-shirt, one slipper. The other is under her desk. Establish location with the caption, not tourist landmarks. This is a fictional room, not Carl's home.

She replays the knockout. “Okay, that was actually disgusting.” She records the wrong part. “Great. Incredible. Professional esports career.” Her first encounter is funny before it becomes intimate.

Three recurring objects replace the combo notebook:

**Headset / work.** A second headset hangs beside “LIA / CUSTOMER CARE.” Taped script: “Thank you for your patience. I understand your frustration.” A visible break indicator explains why she can play. The ghost learns what she does before interpreting her circumstances.

**Dinner / routine.** Rice and egg beside the keyboard, cold. Coffee mug. “Bugnaw na man.” / “It's gone cold.” This is interrupted routine, not a competition in suffering.

**Phone / family.** Let each message open separately. Ate Nena: “Lia, ang abang ugma ha.” / “Lia, the rent is due tomorrow, okay?” Mama: “Sakit na sad imong ulo? Sige man gud kag computer.” / “Headache again? You're always on the computer.” TL Jasmine: “Please review the QA notes after your break.”

Optional Mika thread from the original: “you coming or are you bullying randoms again” / Lia: “one more” / Mika: “you said that 40 minutes ago.” Keep a wall doodle labelled “WORLD CHAMPION OF BEING ANNOYING.”

Read any two of the three main objects to enable Continue; the third remains optional. No exploration timer. Phone is a bounded fictional interface, not a password challenge or investigation. Lia clicks REMATCH and the monitor pulls you back. A Log off option is always available.

Storyboard: room wide → headset and ID → plate → phone → rematch glow. Dain gap: room blockout, three hotspot shapes, prop silhouettes and Lia's amused pose.

## Scene 3 / Still EZ

About 2 minutes. “Same name. Same arena. You still want to win.” A brief exchange gives the player a small moment of success, without using a notebook clue or combo drill. Lia: “okay wait.” Then: “still ez tho.”

She wins the principal match; repeat the same reply pair and timer. Recognition does not disable cruelty or prescribe an answer. Log the choice. A new connection glitch returns you to her room. Do not demand a nice response to progress.

Storyboard: familiar arena → small counter/success → Lia's chat → defeat → glitch. Dain gap: show readable frustration with two or three poses rather than a full combat-animation system.

## Scene 4 / The voice from work

About 4 minutes. Same room, later work interval. Caption: 02:41 AM. The game is idle or closed. Lia wears the work headset. The taped script is the same one you read earlier.

CUSTOMER: “Are you even listening to me?”

LIA: “Yes, sir. I understand your frustration.”

CUSTOMER: “Then fix it!”

LIA: “I'll do my best to help you.”

CALL ENDS. Lia stays quiet. Removes headset. “Okay. Wow.”

“Eight hours of saying sorry for things I didn't do.” Then quietly: “Kapoy na kaayo.” / “I'm so tired.” This is fictional dialogue, not a real recorded customer call.

A brief transition indicates a later break before the game resumes. She reheats the rice and egg, takes a bite and makes a face. “Wow. Five-star dining.” Adds banana ketchup.

Mama's voice note: “Nikaon na ka? Ayaw sigeg kape.” / “Have you eaten? Don't keep drinking coffee.” Lia: “Yes, Ma. I'm literally eating.” She looks at the unfinished plate. “...Technically.” Family pressure exists alongside affection; neither cancels the other.

Revisit the objects: the script is now a voice; the plate is a routine; the phone carries people, not just demands. Show the reply actually delivered on her chat screen. Cruel reply: she exhales and closes chat. Kind: she reads it twice. Silence: no message arrives. Do not rewrite what the player sent.

Retain the original Mika exchange if pacing allows: Mika: “Are you actually coming?” Lia: “One more match.” Mika: “For what? The Olympics of annoying strangers?” Lia: “I could medal.” Mika: “You're impossible.” Lia: “Silver medal, then.” They laugh. She hesitates, then chooses one more match. Her inability to stop mirrors the player's rematching.

She opens the game on her break, wins a short exchange and celebrates: “Let's gooooo.” Types “ez.” Reaches for dinner. The contrast between enforced politeness at work and freedom online is now legible without a lecture. Lia is not simply a sad backstory or a perfect person.

Storyboard: repeat room wide → scripted smile → headset off → quiet beat → reheated meal → family voice note → game returns. Dain gap: reuse room, change three props and two facial poses; no second location needed.

## Scene 5 / GG EZ

About 2 minutes including ending. Final close match. No signature-combo reveal; her ordinary gaming skill is enough. Default route: she narrowly wins, consistent with the rigged premise.

lia_ph: ez.

FINAL MATCH CHAT 00:03. The same “wow, you're good” / “fuck you” buttons. Or silence. No flashback montage, locked choice or glowing correct answer. The player has the same affordance and more knowledge.

Storyboard: arena reprise → narrow defeat → identical chat composition → hold on the player's decision. Dain gap: reuse the first chat panel exactly so the difference belongs to the player.

## Three short endings

These are expanded proposals; the original thread specified three ending routes without finished ending copy.

**A small reply.** “wow, you're good.” Lia replies “gg.” Returns to her night. Her job and rent remain. No magical cure, friendship reward or required gratitude. If the player was always kind: “You didn't need to see the room to choose that. Now you know a little more.”

**Close the window.** Silence or log off. No message arrives. You let the moment pass. “You leave it there.” This is a meaningful boundary, not a failed good ending. Give silence an unhurried visual beat, as the original thread proposed.

**The message lands.** “fuck you” appears on her screen. She closes chat. No breakdown, death or punishment montage. “The message was small on your screen, too.” Offer an optional replay, not a forced correct redo.

All endings: show actual replies, delivery state and elapsed reply time per beat. Optional reflection: “What did you notice?” / “Would you pause before replying next time?” No moral score. Self-report and a designed before/after sequence do not establish lasting impact or causation.

## Prototype handoff

Use roughly 15–20 passages or equivalent simple scenes. Five main scenes plus three endings. Room hubs branch to the headset, dinner and phone, then return. Reuse descriptions conditionally on visit count. Suggested minimal state: replies[], delivery[], visits, seenObjects and timingMode. No inventory economy, combo drills or a separate quest.

Cebuano lines are new draft copy with English glosses. Carl can tune them to his Lapu-Lapu household voice; no independent local-language review is claimed. Keep English customer-service dialogue and natural code-switching. Do not label Tagalog as Cebuano.
